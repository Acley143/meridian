# ADR-0005: Time policy

## Status
Accepted

## Context
A real-time system spanning three languages and a message bus needs a single,
unambiguous representation of time, and needs to be able to measure its own
latency budget (see `docs/nfr-budget.md`) rather than assert it.

## Decision
All timestamps are UTC. `Instant` in Java, timezone-aware `datetime` in
Python (never a naive `datetime`), Avro `timestamp-micros` on the wire. Every
message that flows through the system carries two timestamps:
`event_time` — when the fact occurred upstream (e.g. when a tick was
generated by the market data source) — and `ingest_time` — when this stage of
the pipeline received the message.

## Consequences
- Latency at any stage is `ingest_time − event_time` at that stage, and
  end-to-end latency is directly measurable, not estimated.
- Every producer in the system must stamp `ingest_time` at the point of
  receipt, not at the point of downstream processing.
- Clock skew between machines becomes a measurable, visible quantity instead
  of a silent source of error, because `event_time` always originates at the
  source.

## Alternatives considered
- **Single timestamp field ("timestamp").** Rejected: conflates when
  something happened with when this stage saw it, making the latency budget
  in `docs/nfr-budget.md` unmeasurable end-to-end.
- **Local/wall-clock time.** Rejected outright: multi-service systems across
  machines cannot share a wall clock; UTC is the only sane baseline.
